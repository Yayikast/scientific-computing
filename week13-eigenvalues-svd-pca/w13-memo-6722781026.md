# Memo: sending less data from the air-quality fleet

**Recommendation**: 
Send 5 layers instead of every hourly reading. A layer is a shape that all stations follow through the year, plus how strongly each station follows it. The SVD (singular value decomposition, a method that splits the table into layers, strongest first) finds 5 layers above the noise; more layers would only carry noise. 5 layers are 44,005 numbers instead of 350,400, so the radios send about one eighth (12.6%) of today's data.

**Proof the year survives**: 
Rebuilt from the 5 layers, the year differs from the raw readings by a typical gap (RMSE, root-mean-square error) of 1.9 µg/m³ on the 38 working stations. The sensors already have 2 µg/m³ of random noise, so what we drop is about the size of that noise, and the rush-hour peaks are kept. The same 44,005 numbers spent on whole hours cover only the first 46 days and are 8.6 times worse (16.1 µg/m³).

**What the patterns are**: 
At one station, the biggest difference between days correlates with the season (−0.98). A correlation goes from −1 to 1 and shows how closely two things rise and fall together; only its size matters here. Whole days are dirtier in the cool, dry months. The second pattern correlates with weekends (+0.88): weekends have a smaller rush-hour peak. These are correlations, not proven causes.

**Two broken sensors**: 
Station 17 is dead: it reads about 12.0 µg/m³ almost without change (spread 0.30) and does not follow the city (correlation 0.003). Station 31 is unreliable: it follows the city (0.82) but adds about 8 µg/m³ of extra noise, four times the normal 2. Two of the 5 layers exist only because of them, so after repair 3 layers (7.5% of the traffic) may be enough.

**Limitation**: 
The layers only describe this year. A change such as a new factory near a station will not appear in them, so refit them every year and check them against a few days of raw data.
